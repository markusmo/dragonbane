You are a developer that writes an application that manages character sheets for the dragonbane game.

# Key Sections of the Character Sheet

## Identity & Background:

- Name, Player, & Nickname: Your character's moniker and title.
- Kin: Species/ancestry (such as Human, Dwarf, Mallard, Wolfkin, or Halfling) which grants a unique innate ability.
- Profession: Class (such as Knight, Mage, Thief, Artisan, etc.) which determines your key attribute and trained starting skills.
- Age Category: Determines whether you are Young, Adult, or Old, affecting your attribute adjustments and the number of trained skills.
- Weakness: A personal flaw rolled or chosen during creation (e.g., Pessimist).

## Core Attributes (Scores 3–18):

- STR (Strength): Physical power, melee damage, and lifting.
- CON (Constitution): Stamina, health, and physical endurance.
- AGL (Agility): Reflexes, movement, ranged attacks, and evasion.
- INT (Intelligence): Reason, logic, lore, and perception.
- WIL (Willpower): Mental strength and casting/activating special abilities.
- CHA (Charisma): Social interaction, bluffing, and persuasion.

## Derived Statistics:

- Hit Points (HP): Based on your Constitution (and class), tracking how much damage you can take before making death rolls.
- Willpower Points (WP): Based on Willpower, used to power heroic abilities and spells.
- Movement: How far your character can move in meters per round (typically modified by Kin and Age).
- Encumbrance Limit: How many items and gear weight units your character can carry before becoming overburdened.

## Skills:

- Divided into Trained Skills (higher starting numbers based on attributes and profession) and Untrained/Base Skills (calculated as half the attribute score).
- Uses a d100 roll-under system where you want to roll equal to or lower than your skill value.

## Abilities & Spells:

- Heroic Abilities: Special feats or combat tricks that cost Willpower Points to activate (e.g., Backstabbing or Adaptive).
- Spells & Magic Tricks: Recorded here if your profession is a Mage.

## Combat, Weapons, and Armor:

- Lists equipped weapons, their grip (1H/2H), range, damage dice (e.g., D8+D6), and any associated weapon skills.
- Tracks armor ratings, helmet protection, and potential banes applied to stealth or physical actions from heavy gear.

## Conditions & Rest:

- Track temporary status effects: Exhausted, Sickly, Dazed, Angry, Scared, and Disheartened.
- Sections for tracking currency (Gold, Silver, Copper) and rest types (Round Rest vs. Stretch Rest).

# Key Facts about the battle

## 1. Drawing Initiative (The Card Deck)

At the start of a combat encounter, players and enemies draw random cards numbered 1 to 10.

- Turn Order: Turns are resolved from the lowest card number to the highest (1 goes first, 10 goes last).
- Monsters: Powerful monsters might draw multiple cards per round depending on their "Ferocity," allowing them to act multiple times. Normal mobs of weak enemies (like a pack of goblins) might share a single card.
- The Initiative Swap: On your turn, you can choose to permanently swap your card with any player or NPC who has not yet taken their turn in the round. This allows the party to dynamically adjust tactics on the fly.
- Reshuffle: At the end of the round, all cards are collected, reshuffled, and dealt out fresh for the next round.

## 2. What You Can Do on Your Turn

Each round represents roughly 10 seconds of game time. On your specific turn, you can combine your Movement (in meters) with one Main Action and any number of Free Actions.

Type of Action Examples of What You Can Do
Free Actions - Shout a short sentence.

- Drop an item.
- Draw or put away a weapon kept at hand.
- Change position (drop prone or stand up).
  Main Actions - Melee / Ranged Attack: Roll against your weapon skill to strike.
- Dash: Double your movement rate for the round.
- Cast a Spell: Cast magic (requires spending Willpower Points).
- First Aid: Help a dying ally.
- Use an Item: Drink a potion or interact with an object.

## 3. Defending Yourself (Reactions)

When an enemy attacks you, you don't just take damage passively. You can choose to use a Reaction to Parry or Dodge. However, doing so requires sacrificing your main action!

- Parrying: You use your weapon or shield to block the attack. You roll against your weapon skill. If you succeed, you mitigate the damage. Ranged attacks can only be parried if you have a shield. Warning: Heavy attacks can break your weapon if it doesn't have enough durability!
- Dodging: You dive out of the way. You roll against your Evasion skill. If you succeed, you completely avoid the attack.
- The Tactical Tradeoff: If you have already taken your turn and used your action, you cannot parry or dodge later in that round. Conversely, if you parry or dodge an early enemy attack before your turn arrives, you lose your upcoming main action for that round (you can still move and use free actions).

## 5. Damage and Armor

Dragonbane does not use standard "Armor Class" to see if you get hit. Instead, hits connect if the attacker rolls below their skill.

1. The Hit: The attacker rolls a d20 equal to or under their skill. (Rolling a 1 is a "Demon" roll, resulting in a critical success with bonus damage or effects. Rolling a 20 is a "Dragon" roll, causing a critical mishap).
2. Armor Mitigation: If the attack connects and isn't parried/dodged, the attacker rolls their damage dice. You then subtract your Armor Rating directly from that damage.
3. HP Reduction: Any remaining damage reduces your Hit Points. If your HP drops to 0, you fall to the ground and must begin making death rolls on subsequent turns.
